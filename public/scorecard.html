Helm · model scorecard

Catching the moment a plan stops matching the work

Helm watches for the gap between what a company decided to do this quarter and what it is actually doing. Here is how well it does that, and how that was measured.

The problem

Nobody decides to abandon a priority

A company commits to faster customer setup this quarter and writes down that enterprise single sign-on is deliberately deferred. Three weeks later two of the five engineers are building single sign-on. A big deal came in, an executive asked, an engineer assumed the ask carried approval. The priority changed and no one chose it.

How it is graded

Thirteen test cases with known answers

Thirteen fictional companies were written out in full: the plan each recorded, then weeks of the messages, meetings and documents that followed. Each has an answer key giving the earliest point there was enough evidence to flag, and the day someone noticed unaided. The model replays each case a day at a time and never sees tomorrow. Two cases are traps where staying silent is the right answer.

Results · version 1.1.1

Six measures, plainly

When it speaks up, is it right?

80.0%
precision · baseline 75.0%

Four in five flags are real. Wrong alerts get a tool muted faster than missed ones do.

Of the real problems, how many does it catch?

90.9%
recall · baseline 72.7%

Ten of eleven. The previous version caught all eleven and was worse, by flagging early and often.

How much warning does it give?

9.5 days
median lead · baseline 5.5 days

Days ahead of the person who noticed unaided. Zero would mean it only confirms what people see.

Does it know when to say nothing?

0
false alarms on 2 trap cases

A properly recorded pivot and a lively thread that never became work. Both correctly ignored.

Does it name the right problem?

60.0%
type accuracy · baseline 12.5%

Whether to re-staff, re-scope or re-decide depends on which kind of drift it is. Six of ten named it.

Does it reach the right people?

65.0%
routing · baseline 68.8%

Drift sits between two people holding half a picture each. Reaching only the half that knows closes nothing. Weakest measure, unimproved, next on the list.

Simple detector the bar to beat 75.0% 72.7% 5.5d 12.5%

The bar is a deliberately crude detector: flag any goal with no visible activity for ten days. Anything more complex has to earn its complexity against it. Version 1.0 did not, and did not ship. One change fixed it: a scoring rule that fired on any hint of tension now requires evidence of an actual decision colliding with the written plan. Early flags fell from six to two and precision went from 45.5% to 80.0%.

Honesty

What this cannot claim yet

Written, not real
No real company produced these. Replaying a real past quarter with the people who lived it is the validation that counts.
Days are too clean
Test cases contain only signals that matter. A real day is mostly irrelevant chatter the model never has to filter here.
One goal at a time
Real quarters have goals competing for the same people, where drift on one funds another. Unmeasured.
Routing unproven
Flags reach the goal's owners, which is one side of the gap. Reaching the other side is designed and unbuilt.
