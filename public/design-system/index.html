Helm · design system v2

A considered record,
not a dashboard.

Helm is opened by people who suspect they have lost the plot. This system gives that moment a voice: navy ink on chart paper, instrument-drawn icons, and brass reserved entirely for drift. Verdigris does the second job no one had named: separating what Helm observed from what a person actually said.

Navy — the ink of the record Verdigris — observed, not authored Brass — drift, and nothing else
01

Load-bearing decisions

Every open question in the brief, closed. These are the choices to port first; everything else follows from them.

What this must not look like
The category has one look: dark gradient hero, glassy translucent cards, a single bright accent on near-black, geometric sans, RAG-coloured metric tiles. Helm is deliberately the inverse — warm paper rather than dark glass, printed serif rather than geometric sans, opaque bordered surfaces rather than glow, and no traffic-light tile anywhere. If a screen here could be dropped into a competitor's demo reel unchanged, it is wrong.
Status without color
A stroke vocabulary at the left edge — solid / partial / dotted / absent — plus an instrument glyph: anchor, half-mast, strike, shackle. Brass is reserved entirely for drift. Verdigris carries a second, orthogonal axis: observed by Helm versus authored by a person. It never carries state or severity, so it never competes with brass.
Density
Editorial. 15px base (up from 14), 1.6 line height, 48px table rows, 28px card padding, 72px between sections. A single Compact variant exists for settings tables and nothing else.
One language or two
One — and the app takes the marketing side of it. Navy is the ink, chart-paper warm white is the ground, the compass rose is the product mark, and the icon set is drawn from instruments. Brass crosses over but is demoted from decoration to signal; verdigris is added as the app's own voice, since the marketing site never needed one.
Neutral ramp
Twelve steps, hue 245, chroma rising with darkness (0.005 → 0.044) so every grey is navy-tinted. Surfaces sit warm (hue 85) against it, which is what stops the page reading sterile. Ad-hoc #333–#999 are deleted. muted/secondary/accent collapse into sunken, border and fg-muted.
Typeface
System stack stays for UI. Source Serif 4 is added for one job: outcome titles and page display. The charter is a written commitment; setting it in a serif makes it read as a statement of record and separates it from chrome at a glance. One weight axis, one variable file.
Dark mode
Designed, not inverted. Borders are opaque ramp values (never white/10), surfaces separate by lightness step, and elevation adds a 1px top highlight because shadows do not read on dark ground.
02
The hard problem

Status without color

Five states have to be distinguishable at a glance, in both modes, without red/amber/green, dots or meters. The carrier is the left rule: a 3px stroke inset 0 from the card edge, whose treatment names the state. Position and typography do the rest.

Anchored
Solid rule, full height, foreground ink.
The official direction. Heaviest mark in the system — it is the only state allowed full-strength ink at the edge.
Additive
Partial rule — 38% height, top-aligned.
Entered the quarter without displacing anything. The literally incomplete stroke is the focus warning: it does not reach the bottom of the card.
Replaced
Dotted rule, content at 72%.
Traded away, never deleted. Strikethrough on the title only; the reasoning stays fully legible.
Attached to · Co-sell motion
No rule at all. Indent plus elbow.
Attachment adds no commitment, so it earns no edge mark — only subordination in space. Nesting is the semantics.
Drift · the reserved exception

Brass oklch(0.62 0.07 80) appears in exactly one context: a contradiction between new work and an anchored outcome. It is not a status palette — there is no matching green, no matching red. Its meaning comes from scarcity: if you see brass anywhere in Helm, something you already decided is being contradicted.

Chroma 0.07 keeps it below the "bright status colour" threshold and it is already in production on the marketing site, so nothing new is invented. It never carries the whole message alone — the drift flag is legible in greyscale from its rule weight, its diamond mark and its sentence.

Notice · hairline, 1px
Contradiction · 3px rule
Halt · rule + wash band + "What stops?"

Severity is carried by rule weight and the verb in the sentence, never by a second hue. Three levels, no dots, no score.

Why not chips for all five: a chip puts the state in the content column, competing with the outcome's own words. The edge rule is peripheral — readable while scanning, invisible while reading. Chips remain, but only for facts (quarter, owner, role), never for state.

05

Voice

Helm's personality is not in the wheel or the brass. It is in the fact that the product says "What stops?" instead of "Are you sure?". Everything below exists so that one sentence doesn't sit alone in an interface that otherwise talks like every other tool.

The register, in one line

Helm speaks like a good chief of staff reading back the minutes: factual, unhurried, unimpressed by urgency, and unwilling to let a decision go unnamed.

It is not a coach, not a colleague, not a robot, and never relieved on your behalf.

Eight rules
01
Name the decision, not the click
Buttons say what enters the record — Record this decision, Anchor an outcome, Replace and record — never Save, Submit, OK. If a label works in any product, it is wrong in this one.
02
State the cost in the same breath
Any action that displaces something says what it displaces, in the same sentence, before it happens. Cost is never disclosed in a follow-up toast.
03
Past tense for the record, present for the question
Things that happened are written plainly and dated: Replaced 21 Feb. Things that need a person are asked directly: What stops? Never the two moods in one sentence.
04
No congratulation, no reassurance
Nothing is Great!, All set!, Nice work, or Don’t worry. The record does not have feelings about your quarter. Confirmations state what is now true.
05
No urgency the product does not feel
No Action required, Urgent, Attention needed, exclamation marks, or countdowns. Drift is serious because of what it says, not how loudly.
06
Never blame the person, never blame nobody
Errors name the cause and the next move. Not Something went wrong, and not You entered an invalid ID.
07
Say who noticed
Anything Helm inferred is written in the observing voice and set in verdigris — Observed 3 days ago across 2 sources — never in the same sentence as something a person authored. Helm never says we, and never claims a person's conclusion as its own.
08
One sentence, then stop
Body copy earns its second sentence by adding a fact. Helper text is a fact, not a reassurance. If a sentence could be deleted without losing information, delete it.

Elevation, with stated intent

Four levels. Each has one meaning; a surface may not borrow another level's shadow for emphasis.

e0 · flat
Part of the record
Border only. Charters, tables, timeline entries. Nothing in the record floats.
e1 · raised
Actionable object
1px 2px / 6%. A card you can pick up: draft outcomes, cards with a primary action.
e2 · floating
Transient, dismissible
Dropdowns, popovers, tooltips, toasts. Leaves no trace when closed.
e3 · modal
Requires a decision
Dialogs only, always with a scrim. If it can be ignored, it is not e3.
Dark mode substitutes a inset 0 1px 0 white/5 top highlight plus a lightness step for e1–e2, and keeps a diffuse shadow only at e3.

Focus & accessibility baseline

The app had no focus styles. The treatment is a two-part ring: a 2px ink ring with a 2px background-coloured offset, so it reads on white, on sunken and on navy alike.

Focused link
ContrastBody ≥ 7:1, secondary ≥ 4.6:1, non-text (borders, rules, the drift stroke) ≥ 3:1 in both modes.
Targets44×44px minimum hit area, achieved with padding — icon buttons stay visually 20px but are wrapped to 44.
Never colour aloneDrift always pairs brass with a diamond mark, a rule weight and an explicit sentence. Greyscale must remain fully legible.
AnnouncementsDrift flags render in an aria-live="polite" region; the entry-mode dialog traps focus and returns it to the trigger.

The log layout

The marketing surface is not a second design language — it is the record, set at reading scale. A ruled margin rail carries numbered entries, hairline rows replace cards, and one full-bleed brass moment per page holds the question the product exists to ask. Everything below is now part of the system and applies to any long-form Helm surface: the marketing site, the quarterly record, a printed board pack.

Seven rules
01
The rail
A 104px margin rail runs the full height, separated by a 1px border. It carries a mono entry number and a 46px tick — brass when the entry is load-bearing, border-strong when it is supporting. Content sits 32px in from the rail. The rail never holds prose.
02
Rules, not cards
On log surfaces, structure is drawn with horizontal rules: 2px navy opens a block, 1px hairlines separate its rows. Bordered, rounded, shadowed cards belong to the app; a page of them reads as software, and this reads as a document.
03
Radius by surface
App controls keep the 8px radius. Log surfaces use 2px — buttons included. It is the difference between a control and a printed block, and it is the single change that stops the marketing page looking like a dashboard.
04
Display weight 300
Log headlines are serif at weight 300, letter-spacing -0.028em to -0.035em, line-height 0.95–1.05, and clamp() so they scale with the viewport. Italic is reserved for the turn in a sentence — the clause the reader is meant to feel.
05
One brass moment
Exactly one full-bleed brass band per page, and it must carry the question, not a feature. Brass hairlines may mark individual rows elsewhere; a second brass band spends the scarcity that makes the first one mean something.
06
Soundings
A three-up band of serif figures with a 27ch caption, divided by 1px verticals and opened by a 2px navy rule. Numbers only, no percentages of progress — the caption does the work.
07
Roman enumeration
Lists that enumerate cases or steps use lowercase roman numerals in mono at 11–12px. Arabic numerals stay reserved for entry numbers and real quantities, so the two never blur.

Everything above describes what exists. This describes how the next feature gets designed so it still belongs. Follow it and a screen nobody has drawn yet will already look like Helm.

Step two · the eight rules that do not bend
No new hues
Navy, verdigris, brass, one red. A feature that feels like it needs a fifth colour needs a different structure — weight, rule, position or space. Adding a hue silently breaks brass.
Brass is drift, only
Never a highlight, never a brand flourish, never a hover. One full-bleed brass band per log page, maximum.
Verdigris is attribution, only
Observed by Helm, never authored by a person, never a state or a severity. Always paired with the 7px hollow ring.
No new states
Anchored, additive, replaced, attached, plus drift. If a feature seems to need a sixth, it is almost certainly one of these five seen from a new angle — say which, in the PR.
State lives at the edge
The left rule plus its glyph. Never a coloured chip, never a badge in the content column, never a dot in a table cell.
Elevation states intent
e0 record, e1 actionable, e2 transient, e3 requires a decision. Never borrow a level for emphasis.
Copy passes the voice rules
Name the decision not the click; state the cost in the same breath; no congratulation; no manufactured urgency. Check the message library before writing anything new.
Focus and targets are not optional
Every interactive element gets the two-part ring and a 44px hit area, in the first draft, not the polish pass.
Step three · compose, don't invent

Most new features are an existing composition with different content. Start from the closest one rather than a blank frame.

Showing a commitment
Outcome charter card. Title in serif, facts in the three-up strip, cost as the traded list, provenance in the footer.
Showing a conflict
Drift flag. Two sides named, the contradiction between them, and the question. Severity is rule weight and verb, never a second hue.
Asking a person to choose
Entry-mode dialog. Options as full-width rows, the consequence written under each, the recording verb on the primary.
Showing how something changed
Outcome timeline. Date column in mono, glyph column, prose column. Observed entries in verdigris.
Summarising a period
Record of intent. Navy header with the mark, prose summary, then hairline rows of outcomes with their state at the edge.
Anything long-form
Log layout. Rail, numbered entries, ruled rows, one brass moment.
Step four · when the system genuinely lacks something
One feature needs it
Build it locally from tokens. Do not add it here. Most one-off patterns die with their feature and should not be paid for twice.
Two features need it
It is a component. Add it to this document with a specimen, its states, and one line saying what it is for — and delete the two local copies.
It contradicts a rule above
Then the rule changes, in writing, with the reason. Silent exceptions are how the previous system ended up with three identical greys and amber status text.
You are unsure
Ship the ugly version that obeys the rules over the pretty version that quietly breaks one. The rules are what make the product legible; the polish can follow.
