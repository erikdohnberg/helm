The rules this screen breaks

Every raw-palette colour in the entire codebase — nine utilities — is on this screen or two other spots. Everything else uses tokens.

Drift flag panel
border-amber-500/40 · bg-amber-500/8 · text-amber-600 / dark:text-amber-400
Anchored Chip overridden via className — bg-emerald-500/12 text-emerald-700 / dark:text-emerald-400
bg-green-600 — connection status dot, settings/system
Enter a valid email address. text-amber-200 — waitlist error, on navy
CONFLICT — lib/design/README.md states "no bright status colors" and "muted chips only". This screen uses amber for warning, emerald for anchored, and green for connected. It was not carelessness: it is the one screen that genuinely had to encode status, and muted-only had no answer for it. Treat this as evidence that the rule needs a designed solution rather than stricter enforcement — either a muted-but-legible status vocabulary, or an explicit, narrow exception with defined tokens. Right now it is neither: it is raw Tailwind palette values with no token, no dark-mode pairing for the backgrounds, and an arbitrary /8 and /12 alpha.

Other divergences on this screen

· charter card uses rounded-xl + p-5, where Card uses rounded-lg + p-6
· page title is text-2xl — the only text-2xl page title in the app (others are text-xl)
· body copy is text-base leading-relaxed — the only place app-side prose is not text-sm
· inset panels use bg-muted/40 and bg-background/60 — two more alpha steps
· bullets are a 1×1 rounded-full bg-muted-foreground span, not a list marker
· eyebrow labels here are text-xs uppercase tracking-wide, matching the app, not marketing's text-[11px]
NOTE — read generously, this screen is a prototype of where the whole app could go: more generous type, more air, editorial rhythm. It is much closer to the stated tone rules than the settings surfaces are. It may be the better model to standardise on.
